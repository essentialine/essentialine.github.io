Amazing painting.
I've always loved Canaletto,
but I'm just very curious as to what happens if we trace this.
I just draw the outline of this.
Look how many parallel lines, and
then these perpendicular sides of the buildings.
This is mechanical.

I didn't know Canaletto used a camera obscura.
I have a [camera lucida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camera_lucida).
I should try with perspective like this.
These buildings are still there - I've been to this place as a tourist.
I think these parts of Venice were always meant to be viewed
and appreciated by Venetians, but also by visitors.
Venice was an important international center.

This pier is plain, and drawn wide,
and contrasting to the other buildings.
So, it gives us a position.
It's saying that we're further back behind this pier right in front of us.
This is to give us realism, a sense of being there, reality.
The outline and silhouette give us skyline, the shape, and here the perspective lines,
but nothing else.

## Auction

The pair comes up at Sotheby's London on Wednesday 9 December 2026,
lot 25 of the Old Master
and 19th Century Paintings evening sale,
estimated at 4 to 6 million pounds ([Sotheby's](https://www.sothebys.com/en/buy/auction/2026/old-master-19th-century-paintings-evening-auction-l26036/venice-a-view-of-the-riva-looking-east-venice-a)).
The last time Sotheby's sold them,
in the posthumous sale of Lady Catherine Ashburnham in London on 24 June 1953,
they went as lots 47 and 48 for 2,800
and 3,000 pounds ([Sotheby's](https://www.sothebys.com/en/buy/auction/2026/old-master-19th-century-paintings-evening-auction-l26036/venice-a-view-of-the-riva-looking-east-venice-a)).

Their provenance runs almost unbroken from the easel.
Owen McSwiny, Canaletto's first agent,
had them from the artist in Venice, probably in 1727,
and by September 1730 they belonged to Sir William Morice at Werrington Park in Devon.
The 2nd Earl of Ashburnham bought the whole Morice collection,
forty pictures, for 4,000 pounds after 1786,
and the pair stayed with his family until 1953.
They were sold again at Christie's London in 1986,
and shown at the Metropolitan Museum of Art in New York in its Canaletto exhibition of 1989 to 1990 ([Sotheby's](https://www.sothebys.com/en/buy/auction/2026/old-master-19th-century-paintings-evening-auction-l26036/venice-a-view-of-the-riva-looking-east-venice-a)).

## Background

Giovanni Antonio Canal was born in Venice in 1697,
the son of the painter Bernardo Canal,
and trained with his father
and brother as a painter of theatre scenery.
In Rome in 1720 he worked on the sets for two operas by Alessandro Scarlatti,
and back in Venice he turned to views of the city,
studying with Luca Carlevarijs ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canaletto)).
He owned a camera obscura,
the darkened box that throws an image of a scene onto paper,
but several experts think it gave him ideas rather than lines to trace ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canaletto)).

These two are among his earliest works,
and two of fewer than a dozen he painted on copper.
They were made in the mid-1720s, probably around 1726,
and each is about 45 by 60 centimetres ([Sotheby's](https://www.sothebys.com/en/buy/auction/2026/old-master-19th-century-paintings-evening-auction-l26036/venice-a-view-of-the-riva-looking-east-venice-a)).
McSwiny was
then encouraging him to paint small views of Venice that English visitors would buy ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canaletto)).

The two coppers look in opposite directions from nearly the same spot.
The view east along the Riva is taken from low down,
as if from a boat or a pier, with the Campanile, the Library
and the Doge's Palace on the left,
in an unusually wide panorama.
The view west along the Molo is taken from above,
with the Doge's Palace on the right,
the two columns of the Piazzetta,
and the dome of Santa Maria della Salute across the water.
That second view became the prototype for a composition he
and his followers repeated many times.
The catalogue also notes that he exaggerated how steeply the old Granaries recede ([Sotheby's](https://www.sothebys.com/en/buy/auction/2026/old-master-19th-century-paintings-evening-auction-l26036/venice-a-view-of-the-riva-looking-east-venice-a)).
The Riva degli Schiavoni is named for the Slav merchants from Dalmatia who landed their cargo there,
and runs 850 metres along the water from the Doge's Palace ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riva_degli_Schiavoni)).
