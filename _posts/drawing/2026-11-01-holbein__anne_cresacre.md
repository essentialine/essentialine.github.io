She looks aside toward the viewer, straight, relaxed, her body
in profile.

Pigma Microns,
colored.
Holbein added yellow to her hood and bodice over the black chalk.
Why would two colors of line say more than one?
There are semantic differences that a minimal number of colors
can draw attention to, the dress itself and its slight golden tilt.

Her cross-contour lines wrap
and describe her,
say, on her dress.
Most of a portrait drawing runs along the edges.
The cross-contour, especially when one is obligated to draw the dress,
can help to show the form's variation
in a different axis from the silhouette.
A cross-contour is the line a plane cuts through a curved surface,
like a slice through a loaf.
I think there are lines of the edges of the physical form,
which are primary,
but the inside form can vary in ways the outside cannot reflect,
and then the cross-contour can depict it diagonally, and efficiently.
And then also her collar,
and the very thin edges of her garment.

She sits on a chair in this study,
and in the painting she stands, but the painting burned in 1752.
This is mostly her physical figure,
not the larger story and society.

## Auction

The sheet has never been sold.
It went from Henry VIII to Edward VI,
then through the Earls of Arundel, Lord Lumley
and Charles I, and was back with Charles II by 1675,
says the [Royal Collection](https://www.rct.uk/collection/912270).

<a id="background"></a>

## Background

Anne Cresacre (c. 1511–1577) was the ward of Sir Thomas More,
who took her into his family after her father died.
She was betrothed to his only son, John, in 1527,
and they married in 1529 ([Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Hans_Holbein_the_Younger_-_Anne_Cresacre_(c.1511-77)_-_Google_Art_Project.jpg)).

Holbein drew her soon after he first came to England,
as one of the studies for a large group portrait of More
and his family.
The painting burned in 1752.
A sketch for the whole composition survives in Basel,
and studies of seven of the sitters are at Windsor ([Royal Collection](https://www.rct.uk/collection/912270)).
The fire was at the castle of *Kroměříž* (Kremsier) in Moravia,
and Rowland Lockey's copy of 1592 at Nostell Priory is the only faithful,
same-size record of the lost picture ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sir_Thomas_More_and_Family)).

The chair back at the left of the sheet shows she sat for the drawing,
though in the painting she stands,
near John ([Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Hans_Holbein_the_Younger_-_Anne_Cresacre_(c.1511-77)_-_Google_Art_Project.jpg)).
Holbein drew her in black and coloured chalks,
and wetted the chalk to make the flesh tone of her face ([Royal Collection](https://www.rct.uk/collection/912270)).
