Klimt - her face is very fine.
Her clothing, there's a lot.

And these squiggly pattern lines, well,
I guess there's a bit of dimensionality to these squiggles,
but he didn't take that as a specific way to indicate form.
They have a little bit of form, I think,
but they are also rather flat,
perhaps intentionally, textural.

But these hatching shadow lines,
he's carving out of the background,
so it gives a cleanly finished figure.
These contours and cross contours on her clothing, I think,
they're a real sense of what's needed.
As I drew them, I realized how important they were.

Can't really see her hands,
but I do think that her shoulders are good,
actually, and just the way it's falling.
The shawl makes a tall column from her chin to the bottom of the sheet.
I didn't realize that,
and it contrasts with her thigh's lines from her seated position.
I think it gives the gravity to her seatedness,
and her slight lean is very realistic,
especially with her hands.
Maybe her hand is good, I'll do something there.

By the way, minimal version of this is nice.
I wanted to not hatch like him,
but the brush is sensitive.
If I come in too heavy, I can't build up,
and have a thicker line than I'd want.
But her eyes are really well done,
and I have to be quite careful with the brush pen.

Her cheekbone, it's not clear how to draw that.
He did a little hatching.
I had to draw a thin brush line, but black.
It was all I could do, different expressivity.
So I guess the question of the hair and the cheekbone
is the last and most tricky judgement.

## Auction

This sheet has never come to auction.
It stayed in Klimt's estate (the *Nachlass* stamp is still faintly at the lower right),
passed to Helene Donner in Wien,
and was bought by the collector Rudolf Leopold in 1957,
whose foundation has held it since 1994.

Another of Leopold's Klimt drawings from the same year did come up,
a pencil *Half Nude Sitting Upright*, 1910,
sold at [im Kinsky](https://imkinsky.com/en/auction-results/146/771/7/107830),
Wien, in "The Gustav Klimt Sale" on 24 April 2024, lot 15,
with an estimate of **€150,000–250,000**,
realising **€300,800** with fees.

<a id="background"></a>

## Background

The Leopold Museum places this sheet in a group of portrait drawings from around 1910 that are,
in its words,
"autonomous studies that are not associated with any specific painting".
They were drawings for their own sake, not plans for a canvas.
She sits in profile facing left,
wrapped in a shawl that rises to her chin,
in pencil with touches of white chalk
and red crayon on Japanese paper, 55.8 × 37.1 cm.
The museum's commentary points out the tall,
vertical silhouette of the shawl, stressed by hatching,
and Klimt's habit of going back over finished pencil work with contour lines,
which you can see around the eyes.

The shawl is covered in a floral pattern of circles
and scrolls, and the tracing leaves it out.
What is left is the outline, the profile, the shawl's edge
and the long falling contour of the back.
The pattern lies flat on the paper
and doesn't bend with the body,
so it says little about the form underneath.
The outline and the hatching behind the back do that work.
