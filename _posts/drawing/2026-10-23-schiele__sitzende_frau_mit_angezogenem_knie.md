Just the lines here are so special.
You can really see.
And this fabric also, this shoe, and really the arms.
This arm is done quite well.

Face orientation.
And one could even think that her nose isn't perfect,
yet has a kind of a fluidity to it that is still expressive,
and the whole is beautiful.
He is conveying and communicating something here.
There is a sense of meaning.
She means something to him,
and she is looking at him, perceiving him.

The sheet reads at first as patches of colour,
the green top, the black stockings.
On the contrary, the key points of the original, I think,
are strictly line,
to which he added patches of color, as I do.
I use water pens to pull ink from lines I've drawn,
which can be limiting,
since the line itself remains visible and indicative,
and the pulled color is secondary,
though it can wash beautifully.
Here he pushes color more intensely with gouache.

So that's her silhouette.
I think obviously her eyes, maybe I should have done them first.
The orientation of her eyes is good.
And then just the form of her skull.

Even her thigh has this, the top versus the bottom muscle.
You can see it through the fabric, I think.
He is extraordinary in this.
The top and bottom muscles are indirectly visible.
Her raised knee points almost straight at us,
so the thigh is barely there in outline.
We use our understanding of bones and joints,
perhaps neurological, as with other important features,
to place her body.

One could just leave it here.
His fingers are always...
But then the only problem is that
then we miss some of this fabric,
which is an interesting thing.

<a id="auction"></a>

## Auction

This sheet has never come to auction.
It belongs to the Národní galerie in Praha.
The nearest thing that has is its twin in size, year
and medium,
Schiele's *Selbstbildnis mit kariertem Hemd* (Self-Portrait with Checkered Shirt),
gouache, watercolour and black crayon on paper,
45.5 × 30 cm, 1917.
It came to [Sotheby's New York](https://www.sothebys.com/en/auctions/ecatalogue/2007/impressionist-modern-art-evening-sale-n08359/lot.3.html) in November 2007 as lot 3 of the evening sale,
with an estimate of **$4.5–6.5 million**,
after 75 years in the family of the Viennese dealer Gustav Nebehay.

<a id="background"></a>

## Background

The National Gallery in Praha catalogues this sheet as *Sitzende Frau mit angezogenem Knie (Adele Herms)*,
that is Adele Harms, Edith Schiele's sister.
Other accounts name Edith herself.
The two Harms sisters lived across the street from Schiele's studio in Wien,
he married Edith in 1915,
and both sisters sat for him until his death in 1918.
By 1917 Edith was the model for most of his women,
so the face could be either sister's.

She sits on the floor with one knee drawn up to her face
and the other leg dropped open, in a green top,
a white petticoat and black stockings, on an empty sheet.
Schiele painted it in gouache laid on fairly dry
and scumbled with stiff brushes,
so the colour breaks up over the paper,
and drew the contour around it in black crayon.

The raised knee is a lesson in foreshortening.
The thigh points almost straight at us,
so its real length collapses into a short,
wide shape between the knee and the hip,
and the arm folded over it has to carry the depth.
Where the outline can't show length, it shows overlap,
the hand in front of the knee,
the knee in front of the face.
