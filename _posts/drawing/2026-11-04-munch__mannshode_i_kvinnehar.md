What's interesting here is he is using colour,
cleanly, and he's got a profile on a woman.
I think the expression is actually the key here,
for this one and the previous one.
The woman looks kind of…
basically there are details of her lips and her eyes, gazing down
on him, drawn here, love.
Her hair is the large manifestation of that.
It is distinct here.
Munch sawed the block apart and inked each piece its own colour, so every colour has a hard edge where the pieces met.
This was a good woodcut technique, and one I mean to try out.
I think coloured forms with an edge are very similar to a line, but the line is simpler and less massive.

Her head is in profile and his is frontal, both inside one arch of hair that runs from the top left to the bottom right.
She is looking at him, and if they were both in profile it would have a different feeling.

Looking closely it looks like he's smiling broadly,
which is, for me, unexpected of Munch,
let's put it that way.
So I think something has kind of moved around a little bit. Looking closely, though, I think he is smiling, as is she.

It's surprising how just the tiniest corner of her mouth is a large part of the expression,
just the finest details on the face can affect such a thing.
A small region of a face carries a great deal, and her lowered lids and his stare read to me as love, not as the menace critics usually find in the Salome theme.
I think this has to do with biology. The mouth and eyes are very communicative.

The woodcut of 1896 and Salome, the lithograph of 1903, are the same idea seven years apart.
This one is technically simpler. That one is perhaps even better, as it was after so many years and yet still very close and beautiful.

## Auction

The colour woodcut sold for far more than the later lithograph of the same idea.
At Grev Wedels Plass in Oslo, in December 2015,
an impression signed in pencil, Woll 89, printed in black,
grey and brown on thin Japan paper,
was estimated at NOK 1,500,000–2,000,000
and [hammered at NOK 1,200,000](https://gwpa.no/en/lots/10203).
At the same sale *Salome* (1903) [hammered at NOK 260,000](https://gwpa.no/en/lots/10233).

## Background

Munch cut *Mannshode i kvinnehår* (Man's Head in Woman's Hair) in 1896.
The Munchmuseet lists its tools as gouges, chisel and fretsaw.
A fretsaw cuts right through the block, so the woman's face,
her hair and the man's head can each be inked apart
and printed in their own colour.
In this impression her face is orange-red,
her hair a dark brown, his head bare paper,
and the ground black with the wood grain showing.
The motif is 548 × 382 mm,
and the museum holds 48 objects related to it ([Munchmuseet](https://www.munch.no/en/object/MM.G.00569-26)).
This one was printed by M.
W.
Lassally in Berlin.

Seven years later he drew the same idea on stone as *Salome*,
a woman's head above and a man's below,
both in one mass of hair ([my tracing of it](/drawing/munch__salome/)).
