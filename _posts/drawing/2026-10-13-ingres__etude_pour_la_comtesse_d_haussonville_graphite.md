This is on the portrait of the Comtesse
[I did before](/drawing/ingres__etude_pour_la_comtesse_d_haussonville/).
I was kind of looking through things to try, maybe
because I drew it before, but again this one caught my eye.
This time I'm going to do it in graphite.

I think as usual, I will target a kind of minimalism,
but a different kind of minimalism.
Here I meant minimalism involving graphite.
And down here he's got a lot of drapery, of course.
Only her face gets tone, and the dress stays a contour.
I think it's not a bad plan,
since the face is smoothly rounded shapes
but the dress has linear folds.
I think for this drawing minimalism gives you a good return on your investment.
And just the basic joints, her finger.
I do think the joints are very important.
They are the basic orientation landmarks, I think,
whereas the contours of the muscles give the finer level
of expressivity and realism.

What I thought was interesting was that there is that little bit of shading on her face.
That makes a nice little graphite exercise, I think.
Yes, it's shading for dimensionality.
I picked this one because the shading is sort of light and minimal.
For this one I wanted to go in
and capture and learn to add some of this internal face shading.
It's really, really light and seems to have more value on her nose.
I think Ingres may be line-oriented and think in line a lot,
but clearly his first concern is the shape and form itself.

## Auction

This sheet never went through a saleroom on its way to a museum.
It came to the Fogg at Harvard in 1965, a bequest of Meta and Paul J.
Sachs.
The painting it prepares was sold once,
to the dealer Georges Wildenstein after Paul-Gabriel d'Haussonville's death,
and the Frick Collection bought it in 1927 for $125,000.

<a id="background"></a>

## Background

Jean-Auguste-Dominique Ingres drew this study between 1842
and 1845, in graphite on white wove paper, 23.4 by 19.6 cm,
squared for transfer and signed "Ing" at the lower left.
It is in the Harvard Art Museums (Fogg Museum, 1965.294).
It prepares the *Comtesse d'Haussonville* of 1845,
now in the Frick Collection in New York,
one of an estimated eighty drawings Ingres made for the portrait,
of which fifteen survive.

I traced the same sheet on Sunday 4 October, in line.
