## Auction

No sale of this sheet has been found.
It has been in the Albertina's exhibitions since 1943,
when the museum showed it as number 43 ([Albertina](https://sammlungenonline.albertina.at/objects/3156/edith-schiele)).
In 1918 Schiele had fifty works in the 49th Secession exhibition in Vienna,
and after its success the prices of his drawings rose ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egon_Schiele)).

## Background

Egon Schiele was born in Tulln, in Lower Austria, in 1890,
and Gustav Klimt was his mentor ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egon_Schiele)).
In 1914 he noticed two sisters, Edith and Adele Harms,
who lived with their parents across the street from his studio on the Hietzinger Hauptstraße in Vienna.
Their father was a master locksmith.
He married Edith on 17 June 1915,
and three days later he was ordered to report for army service ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egon_Schiele)).
In January 1917 he was moved to a supply depot in Vienna with little to do,
and he could work again ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egon_Schiele)).
This sheet is from that year.

It is black chalk and gouache, 44.1 by 28 cm, signed
and dated at lower right, "EGON / SCHIELE / 1917",
in a drawn frame ([Albertina](https://sammlungenonline.albertina.at/objects/3156/edith-schiele)).
The Albertina's catalogue says the colour came afterwards,
once the drawing made in front of the model was finished,
and that Schiele coloured hair, dresses
and even house fronts as he liked, not as they were.
Edith was blond and her sister dark,
but in 1917 he drew both, they looked alike,
and the hair colour alone doesn't tell them apart ([Albertina](https://sammlungenonline.albertina.at/objects/3156/edith-schiele)).
The same text sees two ways of working in these portraits,
her face drawn closely from life and her dress stylised,
full colour beside empty paper ([Albertina](https://sammlungenonline.albertina.at/objects/3156/edith-schiele)).
Here the orange coat is laid on with the brush
and has no contour of its own,
and the black bow at her throat is a few flat strokes.

In the autumn of 1918 the Spanish flu reached Vienna.
Edith, six months pregnant, died on 28 October.
Schiele drew her in the three days between,
and died on 31 October,
at 28 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egon_Schiele)).
