This is also just a very soft piece,
so I'm thinking of either the brush pen or just a line.
Cassatt piles colour into the hair, the blouse and the background,
and gives her face only a few soft touches.
Minimalism is one of the things I'm trying to learn. Some Matisse drawings
have it, in a way I'm reminded of here, as does Patrick Nagel, for example.

One dark accent sits where everything else is soft, the black choker at
her throat, and in my tracing it is the heaviest mark on the page.
The choker is a device, at once stylistic, emotional and geometric,
giving the shape of the neck and delineating the head and body.

And also has an ambiguity of the background, those trees, why
are they hazy.
Behind her the pastel has a pale green field, a blue band and a dark
stroke at the left, and my tracing keeps only that one stroke.
It simplifies it. It removes some of the meaning, context and atmosphere,
and replaces them with focus.

The hair is the part I gave least weight to, and that changes where the
face seems to sit.
I do feel I prioritize the face and form over the hair, which I view as
changeable.
For this I thought her dress had contours that depicted her form, along
with her face. However, Cassatt had a kind of intentionality embedded in
her expression, which I don't think I captured, meaning the balance of
eyebrows, eyes and light. I wanted a minimal line and couldn't capture that.

## Background

Mary Cassatt was born in Allegheny, Pennsylvania, in 1844,
and lived most of her adult life in France,
the only American officially associated with the Impressionists ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_Cassatt)).

She found pastel through Degas.
In 1875 she saw his pastels in a dealer's window
and later said she used to flatten her nose against the glass,
and that it changed her life.
Degas introduced her to pastel and to engraving,
and many of her most important works are in pastel ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_Cassatt)).

Four years before this sheet, in 1891,
she showed a set of coloured drypoints
and aquatints made after the Japanese prints shown in Paris the year before.
She liked their simplicity and their flat blocks of colour,
and in those prints she kept to light colours
and avoided black ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_Cassatt)).
