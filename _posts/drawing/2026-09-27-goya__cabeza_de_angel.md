Okay, Goya's *Cabeza de ángel*.
Obviously he's got a lot of shading.
The orientation is straightforward enough,
so I think the lesson here is contour crosshatching.
I'll try some of that,
and with the ink restriction it should work.

Where the hatching goes:
I kept it to a few passages rather than his full build-up;
what I wanted was to see how the form comes out of his strokes.
Leonardo used parallel lines,
which naturally appeals to me ([Leonardo's *Tête de femme*](/drawing/davinci__tete_de_femme_presque_de_profil/)).
This is more complex,
but how Goya does it is very compelling.

It's a low angle.
Crosshatching I think is masterful here, Goya was young.

Looking up:
I rely on the markers and draw portraits from many angles,
and I think my orientation ideas around the face patches were adequate here.
In mine I don't draw the pupils,
yet he still seems to be looking up,
I think from the inner corner of the upper eyelid.

The chin is the interesting problem here:
there's a smooth curve and not a linear edge.
I'm using a finer fineliner than I usually do,
because his hatching is so fine and I think that's an important lesson here,
not just the head geometry, which I think is not difficult.

How the chin came out:
I used a darker value on the chin itself, and I think it pulled off.
But some of my crosshatches went down the jawline
and look like beard wisps, which I didn't intend.

I will draw a thicker line too,
I'm going to mix a little of the thicker line in,
just for certain of these —
for the hair.

Why the hair:
I did like the hair;
I felt it was semantically different,
and it's darker in his everywhere.
Only above the eyelids is as dark in his,
but I didn't want my brush there:
I already had a clean, sharp edge.

This is my second Goya in two days, after [*El coloso*](/drawing/goya__el_coloso/),
which he painted some 36 years later.
To me the power of this one —
the gaze, the emotional intensity, the physicality —
seems very aligned with the colossus.

<a id="auction"></a>

**Auction.** *Cabeza de ángel* has no sale history I can find;
it is a museum sheet.
For scale, Goya's auction record is a pair of 1805 portraits,
Doña María Vicenta Barruso Valdés and her mother,
estimated at **$15–20 million**
and sold for **$16.4 million** with fees
at Christie's New York on 25 January 2023 ([The Art Newspaper](https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2023/01/25/christies-old-masters-new-york-record-breaking-goya-portraits)).

<a id="background"></a>

**Background.** *Cabeza de ángel* (Angel's head) is red chalk (*sanguina*) on paper, 1772,
one of Goya's earliest surviving drawings,
and one of the few he made as a study for a painting ([Museo del Prado](https://www.museodelprado.es/en/the-collection/art-work/angels-head/3370755b-262f-42a9-b3a2-e85cba07f799)).

The painting is *La adoración del Nombre de Dios* (The Adoration of the Name of God),
a fresco of angel musicians and singers
on the vault of the *coreto* (small choir)
of the Basílica del Pilar in Zaragoza ([Universidad de Zaragoza](http://goya.unizar.es/InfoGoya/Obra/Coreto.html)).
It was Goya's first big commission after his return from Italy in 1771.
He painted it in May and early June of 1772,
for 15,000 reales:
Antonio González Velázquez had been paid 25,000 for his ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adoration_of_the_Name_of_God)).

Three of these angel-head studies survive:
this one in the Prado,
one in the Museo de Zaragoza,
and one in the Louvre ([Gobierno de Aragón](https://www.aragonhoy.es/educacion-cultura-y-deporte/aragon-colabora-dibujo-lapiz-goya-7405)).
Of the Zaragoza sheet, the Aragón government notes
"trazos cruzados en el rostro y cuello"
(crossed strokes on the face and neck)
to build the shadows.
