Eva Mudocci, and then, perhaps, Munch himself.
Her head is above and his below, nearly the same size, both caught in the same hair.
The question is how one face comes to dominate the other.
The shapes are contrasting.

Similar style to the one before, and again some of that photographic quality.

Let's see, what colour.
Munch built the print out of black mass and bare paper, and I put colour into it.
I wondered what colour would add to that, or take from it.
I thought colour could add a dimension, and I think it did.

Very long neck, reminds me of Modigliani.
Modigliani stretched necks on purpose, more than a decade later.
Here her lengthened neck, it is elegant, and surreal.

So thick and broad her hair, covering Munch.
I put a thin black outline round her whole mass of hair as well, though the print has only edges there.
The reason I put a thin outline was that I didn't mean to delineate her head or her hair, and Munch was certainly portraying the effect.

This is what would add something to it, it's some kind of brooch.
Maybe some of these slivers in the light are good, just a couple,
to capture some of these undulations that I have.

## Auction

*Salome* comes up at the annual Munch sale in Oslo,
far below the brooch portrait he drew of Eva Mudocci the same year.
At Grev Wedels Plass in December 2015,
a signed impression on thin Japan paper, Woll 245,
was estimated at NOK 250,000–350,000
and [hammered at NOK 260,000](https://gwpa.no/en/lots/10233).
Ten years later the same house sold *Brosjen*, Woll 244 I,
for [a hammer price of NOK 1,250,000](https://gwpa.no/en/lots/18716),
against an estimate of NOK 1,500,000–2,000,000.

## Background

Munch drew *Salome* on the stone in 1903,
the year of *Brosjen* (The Brooch),
and it was printed at the Lassally press.
Twenty-eight impressions are known, some in black,
some in a brownish black ([Munchmuseet](https://www.munch.no/en/object/MM.G.00256)).

He had worked the idea before.
His colour woodcut *Mannshode i kvinnehår* (Man's Head in Woman's Hair),
of about 1896,
also wraps a man's head in a woman's hair ([Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Munch_-_Man%27s_Head_in_Woman%27s_Hair,_MM.G.00569-26.jpg)).
In the Bible, Salome dances for Herod
and asks for the head of John the Baptist.
The Munch scholar Magne Bruteig writes that here she "is removed from her biblical context
and turned into a symbol of perverse womanhood" (quoted by [Grev Wedels Plass](https://gwpa.no/en/lots/10233)).

Like *Brosjen*, it is a lithograph,
drawn in greasy crayon on a flat stone ([how that prints](/drawing/munch__brosjen_eva_mudocci/#background)).
