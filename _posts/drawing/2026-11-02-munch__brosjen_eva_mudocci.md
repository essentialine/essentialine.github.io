Edvard Munch.
The original of course has more, this is a crop.
Her eyes,
especially her brow ridge, and her mouth,
he draws heavier their far edges. Those far
edges are all he really draws of her eyes. 
Dark values at these far edges
often the only thing that's indicated,
when I look in photographs, 
he has a photographic way of rendering her.

We can read a whole face from so few marks, 
Holbein's [Lady Parker](/drawing/holbein__the_lady_parker/), yesterday, is very faint overall, but her eyes are drawn with weight 
towards the center of the arcs of her eyelids.
Under ordinary light, the outer corners of the eye and the line of the mouth are the darkest places on a face.
I think if the light is coming from overhead, the brow ridge often covers the eye sockets, so these distant areas are dark.

How this differs from [The Dance of Life](/drawing/munch-livets-dans-line-and-video/)
and all, more realistic. 

It's called The Brooch.
I think in this print he wanted to depict her physical form, more than the dreamlike figures in those.

Even this shape, this vertical light
shows up in his other works, like the reflection in [*Summer Night's Dream. The Voice*](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Edvard_Munch_-_Summer_Night's_Dream._The_Voice_(1893).jpg)
The undulation of her hair, I don't exactly know how to capture  
his way of using the reflected light and dark to telegraph the form.
This vertical light or figure Munch draws, 
it looks to me like a dream figure.
I think he was using the reflections to capture the undulations and form of her hair.
I was trying to selectively weave them in.

## Auction

Munch printed the stone for years,
and the impressions still come up at the annual Munch sale in Oslo.
At Grev Wedels Plass in December 2023,
an early impression on Japan paper, Woll 244 I,
was estimated at NOK 800,000–1,000,000
and [hammered at NOK 1,650,000](https://gwpa.no/en/lots/16334).
A year later a trial proof inscribed "Forsogstryk 1915",
Woll's fifth state, was estimated at NOK 1,500,000–2,000,000
and [hammered at NOK 1,800,000](https://gwpa.no/en/lots/17824).

## Background

Eva Mudocci was born Evangeline Hope Muddock in Brixton in 1872.
She was a violinist,
played in public first at nine as "Miss Rose Lynton",
and toured Europe with the Danish pianist Bella Edwards.
The composer Frederick Delius, a friend of Munch,
introduced them in Paris in 1903,
the year of this lithograph.
Munch made three lithographs of her,
one with Edwards ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eva_Mudocci)).

The brooch has a story of its own.
Jens Thiis wrote in 1933 that it was traditional Norwegian jewellery
which he gave her at a small party at his home in Trondheim,
during her concert tour with Edwards ([Grev Wedels Plass](https://gwpa.no/en/lots/16334)).

Munch drew her with lithographic crayon ([Munchmuseet](https://www.munch.no/en/object/MM.G.00255-24)).
A lithograph prints from a flat stone, because grease
and water repel each other.
The greasy crayon marks take the ink,
the dampened bare stone refuses it ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithography)).
