## Auction

The board comes up at Sotheby's New York on Wednesday 18 November 2026,
lot 11 of the Blaquier-Arrieta Collection,
a twelve-lot evening sale,
estimated at 3 to 5 million dollars ([Sotheby's](https://www.sothebys.com/en/buy/auction/2026/legendary-the-blaquier-arrieta-collection-evening-auction/femme-assise-de-profil-vers-la-gauche)).
It sits in that sale beside Monet, van Gogh, Gauguin, Degas,
Cézanne
and Renoir ([Sotheby's](https://www.sothebys.com/en/buy/auction/2026/legendary-the-blaquier-arrieta-collection-evening-auction)).
The present owners bought it from the Lefevre Gallery in London in 1977 ([Sotheby's](https://www.sothebys.com/en/buy/auction/2026/legendary-the-blaquier-arrieta-collection-evening-auction/femme-assise-de-profil-vers-la-gauche)).

Its provenance runs through two great collections.
The first owner was Maurice Guibert in Paris.
In 1912 it went to Otto Gerstenberg in Berlin,
and by descent to Margarethe Scharf,
who lent it to the Statens Museum for Kunst in Copenhagen from 1936 to 1950.
In 1951 Albert and Mary Lasker of New York bought it,
and lent it to the Toulouse-Lautrec show at the Museum of Modern Art in 1956 as *Lady in Red* ([Sotheby's](https://www.sothebys.com/en/buy/auction/2026/legendary-the-blaquier-arrieta-collection-evening-auction/femme-assise-de-profil-vers-la-gauche)).
It has been asked for again, for an exhibition of his work,
*Obsessions*, at the de Young in San Francisco
and the Guggenheim in New York,
2027 to 2028 ([Sotheby's](https://www.sothebys.com/en/buy/auction/2026/legendary-the-blaquier-arrieta-collection-evening-auction/femme-assise-de-profil-vers-la-gauche)).

## Background

Henri de Toulouse-Lautrec was born in Albi in 1864
and died in 1901, at 36,
at his mother's château of Malromé ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henri_de_Toulouse-Lautrec)).
He broke his right thigh bone at 13 and his left at 14,
the breaks did not heal properly,
and his legs stopped growing when he was 1.52 metres tall ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henri_de_Toulouse-Lautrec)).
He trained under Léon Bonnat and
then for five years in the studio of Fernand Cormon,
and lived in Montmartre for the rest of his life ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henri_de_Toulouse-Lautrec)).
The Moulin Rouge opened in 1889, the year before this board,
and commissioned his posters ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henri_de_Toulouse-Lautrec)).

He signed this one "HT Lautrec" and dated it "90" at the upper left.
It is oil and *peinture à l'essence* on board,
73.4 by 40.7 centimetres ([Sotheby's](https://www.sothebys.com/en/buy/auction/2026/legendary-the-blaquier-arrieta-collection-evening-auction/femme-assise-de-profil-vers-la-gauche)).
He often laid paint in long, thin strokes
and left much of the board bare ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henri_de_Toulouse-Lautrec)),
and here the hatching of her blouse
and skirt is visible stroke by stroke.
It was probably shown in his lifetime,
at the Salon des Indépendants of 1891
and at Les XX in Brussels in 1892,
under the title *En meublé* (In a furnished room) ([Sotheby's](https://www.sothebys.com/en/buy/auction/2026/legendary-the-blaquier-arrieta-collection-evening-auction/femme-assise-de-profil-vers-la-gauche)).

She sits in a wicker armchair in a red blouse
and a dark skirt, her hands folded in her lap,
her red hair pinned up,
and looks out of the picture to the left.
Behind her hangs a patterned curtain,
and at the left a small table holds a pot.
The sale catalogue does not name her.
The 2012 catalogue of the Gerstenberg collection titles the picture *Sitzende Frau im Profil von links (Jane Avril)* ([Sotheby's](https://www.sothebys.com/en/buy/auction/2026/legendary-the-blaquier-arrieta-collection-evening-auction/femme-assise-de-profil-vers-la-gauche)).
