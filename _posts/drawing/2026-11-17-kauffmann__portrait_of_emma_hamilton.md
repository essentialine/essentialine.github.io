So, beautiful anatomy shading.
Her eyes are deep, I wonder if I can actually capture that expression.
I keep her eyes more masklike. I often admire ancient statues.

Her eyelids are very fine, but hard to trace through the paper,
especially as tracing such a precise actual structure of her eyelid.

I was thinking for a
while there's no point in using the brush variation at all.
While that's true, and
while it can cause your drawing to go wrong quickly,
to put a heavy mark where you didn't want one,
it brings immediacy.

I have some shadings left, so I try to get away with the lighter colour.
Soften them a little bit with the water brush.
Water dissolves some of the original Pilot SVS strokes too.

## Background

Angelica Kauffmann was born in Chur,
in the Swiss Graubünden, in 1741,
and trained as assistant to her father,
the muralist Joseph Johann Kauffmann.
She was known as a painter by the age of twelve.
In 1768 she was one of the founding members of the Royal Academy in London,
one of only two women among them, with Mary Moser.
After her marriage to the Venetian painter Antonio Zucchi in 1781 she settled in Rome,
where Goethe was among her friends,
and she died there in 1807 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angelica_Kauffman)).

Her sitter was born Amy Lyon in 1765, a blacksmith's daughter from Cheshire.
As Emma Hart she sat for George Romney many times,
and in 1786 she was sent to Naples, to Sir William Hamilton,
the British envoy.
There she became famous for her *Attitudes*,
poses taken from classical art and held in a few shawls,
which Goethe saw and described.
Hamilton married her in London on 6 September 1791,
and she returned to Naples that autumn as Lady Hamilton ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emma,_Lady_Hamilton)).
Kauffmann drew her that same year, in black
and white chalk on grey prepared paper ([The Met](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/359453)).

The grey paper does the middle tones,
so the chalk is needed only at the two ends of the scale.
Black chalk goes into her curls, the sockets of her eyes
and the shadow under her jaw,
and white chalk picks out the light on her forehead
and down the bridge of her nose.
