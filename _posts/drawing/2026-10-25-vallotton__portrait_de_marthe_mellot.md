A work by Vallotton.
Crisp, clean lines.
And there's something that's anatomically good.
There's something kind of like a step back in a way.
Even though it's quite realistic,
one kind of gets the sense that there's something else going on here.
Her expression is more real than her depiction,
which is also somewhat real but flatter and more diagrammatic,
if still 3D.

The thing that struck me was her pupils are so shiny,
which is not going to be easy for me to do,
there's a little sparkle right in the middle of them.
Obviously her eyes have that, you know,
just roundness to them.
I don't think I was able to resolve it here,
especially for such a small element as her pupil.

I'll start with a fine line.
There's just a silhouette,
let's draw her ear in,
her hairline,
her black dress.
I have my brush.

There's this diagonal line that is working.
It was a bit of a cross-contour type idea,
but with the convenience of drapery.
The use of the subtle blacknesses in her dress again.
Her curls are a complicated way to actually communicate her form.
At first I thought they were irrelevant to the form,
then I realized they were not.
They were carefully shaped,
so I tried to trace them,
translating the brush variation
to try to deal with his tonal variation.

I'm really using the reflections in a lot of things.
Her hair also has that shine to it.
Just a black and white,
but you have to set some kind of baseline or something to get something like that.
I remember my [Ferriss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugh_Ferriss) tracing.

I did try to add more black there
and so perhaps separate larger swaths of light versus dark,
but not in the specific way he is doing.

## Auction

This portrait has never come to auction.
According to the [Kunsthaus Zürich](https://collection.kunsthaus.ch/en/collection/item/3819/),
it was commissioned by Marthe and Alfred Natanson,
probably in 1898, stayed in Vallotton's estate,
went to the Galerie Paul Vallotton in Lausanne,
and was bought in 1938 by the Vereinigung Zürcher Kunstfreunde out of that year's Vallotton exhibition at the Kunsthaus,
which has shown it on loan from the association ever since.

<a id="background"></a>

## Background

Marthe Mellot (1870–1947) was an actress at the Théâtre de l'Œuvre in Paris.
In 1901 she married the writer Alfred Natanson,
whom she had met through Édouard Vuillard.
Alfred and his brothers Alexandre
and Thadée had founded *La Revue blanche*.
The Kunsthaus calls the theatre
and the magazine "two central institutions of the Parisian avant-garde around 1900".
Vallotton painted the couple again in 1906, as a pair.

Vallotton joined the Nabis in 1892
and made his name first with woodcuts,
starting with a portrait of Paul Verlaine in 1891.
The woodcuts are built from "large masses of undifferentiated black
and areas of unmodulated white",
and his paintings of these years use flat areas of colour,
hard edges and simplified detail.
The same year as this portrait,
*La Revue blanche* published *Intimités*,
his ten woodcut interiors.

The black dress here works like one of those woodcut masses,
a single dark shape against the brown wall,
with the appliqué scrolls on the bodice set in only a step lighter,
so the pattern reads perhaps as a flat design laid on the silhouette
rather than as cloth turning around a body,
or perhaps, in my interpretation, as in fact
a conveyance of her form.
The tracing has to choose which edges of that dark mass to keep as lines.
