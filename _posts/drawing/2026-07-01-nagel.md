![Tracing after Nagel, 7.1](/assets/images/nagel/260703fr.nagel.rot90cw.png)

Just tracing [Patrick Nagel](https://patricknagel.com/). Super interesting.

I liked him as a teenager — it was the 80s, his heyday, and I was surprised by how
expressive he was. Now he brings me back to those days: humid New Jersey summers,
middle and high school. He carries some of that imagery with him, and maybe my
tracing carries a little of the 80s too.

I see in his work his solutions, simplifications, idealisations
of problems I've been intrigued with — very similar questions of the line.
The instrument of the eye is the most important and interesting place to start.
It's the problem of what's making this beautiful — what lines I can draw, what's
the geometry of the subject, what that has to do with the beauty of a mountain or
a tree.

Very little other form. Contours, cross contours.
This thing is very, very focused.

He draws her hair with simplicity I've always admired.
He expresses the hair — but not with a lot of lines.
A certain amount, and then he's relying on a vision of it, keeping the edge and
the beauty, harmonious with the face.
Very broad chunks.
He draws white lines in the hair that I can't.
A few internal reflections, but he gets the idea across nicely.

The nose: super delicate. Just a little bit.
And those famous red lips — their own field.

With just these few lines, perhaps we get some foundation to Nagel.

Under the ear, there is a shadow.
The expressiveness of just the eyes.
Geometric shapes behind — a rectangle; she's lying on something.
There is shadow, helping form, but perhaps even that's not the point.

There's a neurological basis for that, I think — [face patches](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/how-the-brains-face-code-might-unlock-the-mysteries-of-perception/)
in the brain, with cells tuned to specific features like the distance between
the eyes. The eyes are just wired to matter more.

The major lesson is the eyes.
