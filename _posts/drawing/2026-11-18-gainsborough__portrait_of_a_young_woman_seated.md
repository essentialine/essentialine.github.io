And then we have, interestingly, a neck choker.

The water brush is quite useful for some of this.

## Auction

No sale of this sheet with a published estimate and price has been found.
The Metropolitan Museum of Art bought it in 2012 with gifts from Lila Acheson Wallace
and the Christine Burgin and Donald Young Foundation ([The Met](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/405840)).
His portraits rose quickly in value in the 1850s,
once Lionel de Rothschild and
then other Rothschilds began to buy them ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Gainsborough)).

## Background

Thomas Gainsborough was born in Sudbury, in Suffolk,
in 1727, the son of a weaver.
At thirteen he went to London
and trained under the French engraver Hubert Gravelot.
In 1759 he moved his family to Bath, to 17 The Circus,
where he studied the portraits of van Dyck
and found the fashionable sitters he had lacked in Ipswich.
He was a founding member of the Royal Academy in 1769,
and moved to London in 1774 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Gainsborough)).
The Met dates this sheet to about 1760,
the first Bath years ([The Met](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/405840)).

He did not much like the work that paid.
In a letter of the 1760s he wrote that he was "sick of Portraits"
and wished to take his viola da gamba to some sweet village
and paint landscapes ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Gainsborough)).
He was known for speed,
and he worked from what he saw more than from academic rules ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Gainsborough)).

The sheet is black chalk with touches of red wash,
29.8 by 17.8 cm,
its corners trimmed ([The Met](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/405840)).
Her face is drawn with care,
her eyes turned out to the viewer,
and the ribbon round her neck is a single dark band.
Below her shoulders the chalk loosens.
The bows down her bodice, the ruffles at her elbows
and the skirt over her lap are scribbled fast,
with lines that cross and double back,
and the chair behind her is a few strokes.
